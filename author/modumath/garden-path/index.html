Why add the path twice?

A rectangular garden measures 12 m by 8 m. A path 1.5 m wide runs around the outside. How much ground does just the path cover?

Why is the outside length 15 m?

Trace a horizontal line through the middle. It crosses 1.5 m of path on the left, then 12 m of garden, then 1.5 m of path on the right. The outside width grows the same way, from 8 m to 11 m.

Check the area of the path

The outside rectangle covers 15 × 11 = 165 m². Subtract the garden's 12 × 8 = 96 m². The path covers 69 m².

Try the step again

This time the garden is 10 m by 6 m and the path is 1 m wide. Before opening the answer, write down the two outside dimensions.

Check your dimensions and area

The outside dimensions are 12 m and 8 m. The path covers (12 × 8) − (10 × 6) = 36 m².

Building it for a phone

We're a math teacher and a developer. We used Claude Code to build the three parts of ModuMath: enter your own problem, unfold the explanation at the step you need, and try a similar problem. We use a separate check for the generated question and its answer.

One small bug made the explanation hard to read on a phone. A long equation stretched the whole list of steps past the edge of its card. Giving the grid a minmax(0, 1fr) column and letting its children shrink kept the list on screen. The student can now open one step without losing the others off to the side.

Try ModuMath with a problem of your own. The pilot includes limited free practice.
